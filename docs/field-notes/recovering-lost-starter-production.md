## Question

How can a roster recover the contribution of an unavailable premium starter without unnecessarily dismantling its remaining strengths?

A lost player changes coverage, uncertainty and available paths. Original draft cost alone does not establish the size of today's loss. An in-season repair is distinct from a dynasty rebuild: the appropriate horizon depends on league format and the operator's objective, not the injury by itself.

## Declare the problem before choosing a transaction

Record the intended horizon, scoring, required starters, flex eligibility, bench/reserve limits, deadlines, roster snapshots and permitted acquisition resources. Distinguish:

- an uncovered mandatory starting slot;
- a weak replacement already covering that slot;
- insufficient injury/bye alternatives;
- lost upside despite adequate ordinary coverage.

Use the actual reachable replacement as the comparison. Do not charge a repair path the injured player's full production while ignoring the replacement that could otherwise start. A roster sum is not a legal starting-lineup comparison. Draft prestige, RB counts and bench size do not establish expected starter production.

## Four repair paths to investigate

| Path | Structural question | Important failure case |
|---|---|---|
| Use an existing replacement | Is the newly available work demonstrated, and which starting slot can it cover? | An opening is assumed to create a full role before redistribution is observed |
| Trade from another position's depth | What remaining starters and contingencies are displaced by the outgoing asset? | A nominal surplus is actually needed for flex, byes or another injury |
| Exchange one premium asset for multiple players | Does each incoming asset improve a usable slot after the outgoing player and any explicit cut are removed? | Two incoming bench assets are mistaken for two starter improvements |
| Acquire an emerging role | Can the roster afford uncertainty and a wait for evidence? | A speculative role is used as though its workload were secure |

This is an unordered set of alternatives, not a preferred sequence. A combined path is possible but requires accounting for its complete costs.

## Synthetic package example

A roster must start two RBs and has an optional RB/WR/TE flex. One RB is unavailable. A hypothetical exchange gives up the healthy RB-A for RB-B and RB-C.

Compare complete legal lineups for:

- hold RB-A and use the currently reachable replacement;
- exchange RB-A, start RB-B/RB-C, and remove an explicitly selected player if capacity requires it;
- retain RB-A and repair the shortage using a different outgoing asset.

Record what happens to flex coverage, bench alternatives, acquisition resources and shared injury/team exposure in each case. Do not choose the required cut silently. A two-for-one package does not establish an improvement, legality, feasibility, willingness or fair value.

Without governed player distributions, stop at mechanical coverage and stated uncertainty. Expected points, floor, ceiling and value over replacement are unavailable rather than computed from invented inputs. A counterparty roster may show structural fit, but not acceptance probability or manager psychology; no league-chat reading or manager ranking belongs here.

## Research protocol for later authorized execution

1. Define a comparable cohort of roster disruptions using an ex-ante eligibility rule, a fixed league contract and a declared decision horizon. Keep redraft and dynasty results separate.
2. Freeze each decision's available evidence and realistic acquisition set before looking at subsequent outcomes. Preserve the no-transaction baseline.
3. Keep operator-proposed packages, observed submissions and completed transactions separate. A proposed acquisition is not an asset actually obtained.
4. Measure mandatory-slot coverage separately from subsequent legal starting-lineup outcomes. Specify lineup policy, injury handling, roster cuts, resource cost, schedule and observation window before execution.
5. Keep unsuccessful repairs, rejected/unavailable acquisitions, unchanged rosters and abandoned hypotheses. Missing outcomes stay missing.
6. Separate observational associations from causal claims. Managers who choose different repair paths may differ in resources and roster quality; a better outcome alone does not prove the path caused it.
7. A replay requires contemporaneous rules and acquisition availability. A season projection sum or hindsight-selected trade partner cannot substitute.

Candidate research outputs are conditional comparisons, including failure cases, not a universal rebuilding method. Numeric evaluations require separately governed inputs and consumer calibration; none are computed here.

## What would challenge the framing?

- Apparent depth does not improve any actual legal starting slot.
- Repair costs remove equally important production or future alternatives elsewhere.
- A supposed role beneficiary does not receive the redistributed work.
- The usable acquisition set is materially worse than the proposed set.
- A path succeeds only under hindsight-selected replacements or optimistic availability.
- Conclusions change materially with scoring, flex demand or horizon.
