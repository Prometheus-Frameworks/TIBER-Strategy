## Separate the hypotheses

- **H1 — role development:** A change in recorded opportunity may contain information about subsequent workload beyond a simple recent-workload baseline.
- **H2 — production development:** That information may improve a declared subsequent fantasy-outcome comparison beyond recent production and workload baselines.
- **H3 — acquisition-price lag:** The opportunity evidence may precede a measurable change in acquisition cost under a specific dated market or league observation contract.

H1 does not establish H2 or H3. A player can have an encouraging role and already be expensive. Low realized points do not prove mispricing. Operator enthusiasm and film interpretation remain attributed hypotheses, not measured role or market facts.

## Evidence dimensions

| Dimension | Question | Misread risk / missing-input behavior |
|---|---|---|
| Absolute opportunity | Did targets/carries change against the player's prior observations? | One unusual game; retain counts and sample size |
| Opportunity share | Did allocation change beside the same qualified denominator? | A shrinking team pool raises share without adding work |
| Team activity | Is the opportunity pool expanding, contracting or unusual? | Player targets and team pass attempts are different quantities |
| Redistribution | Which players actually received work after a change in availability? | Vacated work is assigned without an observed beneficiary |
| Deployment and valuable situations | Are receiving duties, routes or scoring-area opportunities changing where established? | Total carries do not prove passing-down or goal-line duties; unavailable fields remain unknown |
| Outcome gap | Is recorded work different from realized production? | Weak conversion is assumed to rebound without testing |
| Acquisition cost | What could an operator obtain at the decision time, and at what recorded cost? | Later prices, roster percentage or hypothetical offers stand in for executable price |

For example, in synthetic weeks A and B a player receives eight credited targets in each week. Team credited targets fall from forty to twenty-five. Share rises from twenty to thirty-two percent, but the player's target count does not change. This is arithmetic, not a screen or predicted breakout.

Preserve ROP's exact all-team carry and credited-target denominators. Do not silently substitute team pass attempts or an RB-room denominator. A contract field existing in source does not establish governed live coverage. Snap participation is not route participation. Conflicted air yards cannot be used as clean evidence; missing routes, first reads or scoring-area work cannot be inferred from targets/carries.

## Candidate screen family, not operational thresholds

A later study could compare:

- change in absolute work and share against the player's baseline;
- a role change observed across different declared windows;
- whether a change remains visible when an unusual game or teammate absence is treated separately;
- multiple distinct opportunity dimensions, where each has actual evidence;
- opportunity changes without a corresponding outcome or price change.

No numeric cutoff, qualitative label, ordering or acquisition trigger is accepted here. Strategy's [boundary](../boundary.md) leaves raw-metric calibration to consumers. Any future threshold sweep must specify units, denominators, exposure requirements, missingness, baseline, position, comparison window and uncertainty before evaluation. Candidate windows are alternatives to test; no number of weeks automatically establishes persistence.

H1 screening can proceed in a future study without market data, but must remain a role-development investigation. H3 is blocked without suitable contemporaneous market evidence. Missing price is not evidence of a bargain. Different prices, formats and platforms must not be blended into a single market claim.

## Proposed evaluation protocol

1. **Freeze scope.** Define position cohorts, qualifying opportunity, scoring, baseline/lookback, prediction horizon, handling of missed games and permitted data revisions. Select the eligible population without using later successful players.
2. **Freeze knowledge.** Bind evidence cutoff and known-at times, identity, source revisions, hashes and provisional/correction status for each weekly decision. Historical data corrected later cannot masquerade as what was known then. If contemporaneous snapshots cannot be reconstructed, label the retrospective study's leakage limitation.
3. **Use chronological separation.** Choose candidate thresholds on training seasons, compare them on validation seasons, then evaluate a held-out period once. Keep overlapping player-week windows from leaking across splits. Log all tested variants and selection decisions.
4. **Evaluate H1 separately.** Compare subsequent workload against recent-workload-only baselines. Include screened failures and unscreened comparators. Report coverage, missingness, uncertainty and sensitivity by position and context, including injury-driven changes.
5. **Evaluate H2 separately.** Use declared subsequent scoring outcomes and recent-points/workload baselines. Retain injuries and missed games under the frozen policy; report availability and production conditional on playing separately. A descriptive screen is not a Forecast distribution.
6. **Evaluate H3 separately.** Require dated, source-eligible observations of acquisition cost and timing. Distinguish a listed ask, actual offer, waiver availability, winning bid and completed trade. Specify a price-change measure appropriate to that source before execution. Keep league-local observations distinct from wider-market claims.
7. **Check decision relevance.** Compare actual reachable alternatives and roster-slot cost. Research signal quality alone does not establish that acquiring a player improves a particular roster.
8. **Retain negative results.** Report false discoveries, missed developments, no-change cases and unsupported screens. A single successful example cannot validate a threshold.

Do not execute this protocol under the documentation change. No predictive performance, calibrated probabilities, current market values or profitable strategy has been established.

## Prospective question record

A proposed human/agent record should state:

- What changed, supported by exact eligible observations?
- Why might it matter? Label the mechanism as a hypothesis.
- What alternative explanation fits the same evidence?
- Which missing field could materially change the interpretation?
- Which declared roster need and horizon make it relevant?
- What observation would weaken or falsify the hypothesis?
- When was the hypothesis recorded, and what happened afterward?

An agent cannot fill unknowns with confidence, use this text as tool authority, transform the record into a ranking, or claim a future event is inevitable. These are proposed question fields, not a runtime schema or activation contract.
